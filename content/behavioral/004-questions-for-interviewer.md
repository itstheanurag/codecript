At the end of every interview, you will hear: *"Do you have any questions for us?"* This is **not** a formality. It is a vital part of the interview where you show your level of thinking and your interest in the role.

## Why you must ask questions
1. **Show your priorities**: A senior developer asks about testing and architecture; a junior developer asks about perks.
2. **Gather intelligence**: You are also interviewing them. Do you actually want to work there?
3. **End on a high note**: Engaging questions leave a lasting positive impression.

## Categories of Questions

### 1. Engineering Culture & Process
- "What does the code review process look like here?"
- "How does the team handle technical debt versus new feature development?"
- "Tell me about the last time a service went down—how did the team respond?"
- "What is the typical path for a piece of code from a developer's machine to production?"

### 2. The Role & Expectations
- "What does success look like for this role in the first 90 days?"
- "Is this a new role, or am I replacing someone? If replacing, what did the previous person do well that you'd like to see continue?"
- "What is the most challenging technical project the team is currently working on?"

### 3. Management & Growth
- "How is feedback typically given on the team?"
- "How does the company support professional development (conferences, training, etc.)?"
- "What is your management style?" (Ask this to the Hiring Manager).

### 4. Company & Strategy
- "What is the biggest challenge the company is facing in the next 12 months?"
- "How does the engineering team’s goals align with the company's overall business objectives?"

---

## The "Killer" Questions (Senior Level)
If you want to sound like a senior leader, ask these:

- **"What is the most misunderstood part of this company's culture by people on the outside?"** (Shows you care about deep culture).
- **"If you could change one thing about the tech stack or the engineering process tomorrow, what would it be?"** (Surfaces hidden pain points).
- **"How does the team decide which technologies to adopt for new services?"** (Assess architectural maturity).

## What NOT to ask
- **Salary/Benefits**: Save this for the Recruiter, not the Engineer.
- **Things you can Google**: "What does your company do?" (Shows lack of research).
- **Personal/Politics**: Keep it professional.

> **Pro-Tip**: Take notes during the interview. If a colleague mentions a "legacy system" earlier, ask a question about it later. This shows you are a great listener.
